## Summary

Revenue is **$10.85M against $11.82M planned — $969K behind, 8.2%**. That is the
year, not the week.

Seasonality matters to how the rest of it reads. Straight-lining the plan puts
the year at $13.44M and net income at **−$469K**. The seasonality-adjusted trend,
which weights Q4 the way the business actually earns it, puts revenue at
**$13.66M** and net income at **−$245K**. Both are short of a planned **+$196K**
profit, but the honest number for the year is a **$441K miss, not $665K**.

COGS is $459K under plan and OpEx $124K over. Neither is the story on its own —
see below.

## COGS & Shipping

$459K under plan in dollars, and the rate moved the other way: **77.0% of revenue
against 74.6% planned**, 58.9% against 56.2% with labor stripped out.

That is the merch plan showing up, not a buying failure. The buy was committed
before the season; when revenue lands 8% short, the goods do not disappear, they
move into next year's Q1 at a discount. The discount is what pulls the rate down.
The same cycle as prior years.

## Inventory

**Week 40 has not been counted. The figures below are week 39**, which is the
last reading on the tab.

**COG on hand $2.03M against $1.96M at the same point last year, +3.6%. Units
133,176 against 116,289, +14.5%.** Average unit cost is $15.25 against $16.87 —
down 9.6%.

More units at lower cost, carrying 14.5% more pieces into the back half on 8%
less revenue. Spring 26 sell-through is running **89.6% against Spring 25's
97.9%**. Both point the same way: the Q1 carry is larger this year than last,
not smaller.

This is the piece that has no projection behind it. An EOY inventory projection —
on-hand plus committed receipts less sell-through at the seasonality curve —
would turn the Q1 carry from something we infer into something we can read, and
it is the input the cash flow carryover equation is currently missing.

## Labor

**$1.97M against $2.17M planned — $201K under.** Nine of twelve months
in, every month except August came in under. It is the one cost line running
genuinely favourable rather than favourable-because-of-volume, and it accounts
for 44% of the whole COGS underspend.

## OpEx

$124K over plan, and **28.1% of revenue against 24.8% planned**.

Almost all of the overrun is one line. **Advertising is $470K against $353K
planned, +$118K** — 95% of the total OpEx variance, projected to finish $643K
against a $525K plan. The rest is small and mixed: Bad Debt $40K against nothing
planned, INK merch profit share $21K against nothing, Equipment & repairs $16K
over, offset by Guaranteed Payments $60K under.

Worth holding those two facts together: advertising is the overrun, and DTC is
the only channel beating plan, by 28% on trend. That is not proof the spend is
causing it, but cutting advertising to close a $118K gap would be aimed at the
one channel that is working.

## Cash Flow

**Cash in $11.09M against $11.54M in the same weeks of 2025 — down $450K, 3.9%.
Cash out $11.29M against $11.35M — down $57K, 0.5%.**

Net cash is **−$197K this year against +$196K last year**, a $393K swing. Taking
in less and spending the same.

That is after drawing **$975K on the credit line and paying back $125K** — a net
$850K draw. The year has consumed $197K of cash *and* $850K of line.

The Columbia line sits at **$2.55M against a $1.7M EOY goal — $850K still to
pay**. Available cash adjusted is $615K, which leaves a **$235K shortfall**, and
that lands on A/P - CNS as carryover into 2027. That is the equation working as
intended: the Q1 discount sell-through is what retires it, which is why the
inventory carry above is the number that decides it.

Bank balance is $162K, up from $61K at week 39.
